    <p>There are different rules for who pays for the ongoing cost of utilities and services, depending on what they are.</p>
<p>You can also read about <a href="/housing/renting/starting-and-changing-rental-agreements/applying-signing-and-moving-in/setting-up-utilities-and-services">setting up utilities and services</a>.</p>

<p>Other pages have information about <a href="/housing/renting/repairs-alterations-safety-and-pets/gas-electrical-and-water-safety-standards/efficiency-standards-for-replacement-appliances-and-fixtures">replacing water, gas and electrical&nbsp;appliances</a></p>
<h2><a name="water"></a>Water and sewerage</h2>
<p>If the property has its own meter, the renter must pay for the water they use, as well as the sewage disposal charge.&nbsp;This applies unless the rental provider (landlord) agrees to pay. If the rental provider agrees to pay, it should be in the rental agreement (lease) as a special condition.  <br />
<br />
The rental provider is still responsible for charges relating to the sewerage infrastructure connecting to the house. <br />
<br />
If the property doesn&rsquo;t have its own meter, the rental provider must pay. <br />
<br />
The rental provider must pay all other charges related to water supply and sewerage or drainage services. This includes all charges relating to the pumping out and cleaning of sewerage tanks, unless the renter caused the damage. Different rules may apply when a tank is the main source.</p>
<p>The rental provider must pay for cartage charges for:&nbsp;</p>
<ul>
    <li>drinking water, unless the charges are related to the amount of water supplied to the rented premises during the renter&rsquo;s occupation&nbsp;</li>
    <li>refilling fire safety water tanks.&nbsp;</li>
</ul>
<h2><a name="electricity-gas-and-oil"></a>Electricity, gas and oil</h2>
<p>The rental provider must pay to have the electricity, gas or oil supply installed and connected.&nbsp;</p>
<h3>Bottled gas</h3>
<p>If the property uses bottled gas the renter pays for the supply and hire of gas bottles.&nbsp;</p>
<h3>Metered electricity, gas or oil</h3>
<p>If the property has its own meter for electricity, gas or oil, the renter must pay all supply and usage costs, unless the rental provider agrees to pay. In that case, it should be in the rental agreement as a special condition. If the property doesn&rsquo;t have its own meter, the rental provider must pay.</p>
<h3>Social housing heating and laundry costs</h3>
<p>People in social housing may be charged separately for expenses such as heating and laundry.&nbsp;</p>
<h2><a name="phone-and-internet"></a>Phone and internet</h2>
<p>Renters must pay for the ongoing usage costs for phone and internet, unless the rental provider agrees otherwise.&nbsp;</p>
<p>If the renter wants to set up phone and internet connections, they will have to ask the rental provider&rsquo;s permission.</p>
<p>There are two possible costs for connecting a property to a fixed telephone line (landline) for phone or internet use:</p>
<ul>
    <li>a line connection fee, and</li>
    <li> a general connection fee.&nbsp;</li>
</ul>
<p>A general connection fee is charged when there is an existing phone line at the&nbsp;property&nbsp;but it is not currently connected.&nbsp;</p>
<p>A line connection fee is what the telephone company will charge if a telephone line has not previously been set up at the property. This may be the case if the property is new or has been empty for some time (renters should check this before they sign the rental agreement).&nbsp;</p>
<p>A renter who wants to set up a connection to an existing phone line at the property must pay the general connection fee. They will usually be charged for this in their first telephone bill. This applies unless they have a different arrangement with the rental provider. <br />
<br />
If there is no phone line or internet connection at the property, you can talk to the rental provider about installing one.  <br />
<br />
The rental provider cannot refuse a request for phone or internet connection without a good reason.  <br />
<br />
The rental provider must pay all costs and charges for the initial installation of phone or internet connections. This includes connection to the National Broadband Network (NBN).&nbsp;</p>
<h2><a name="television"></a>Television</h2>
<p>Television is not an essential service for rental properties. Renters who want to use a TV should check whether the property has a digital TV antenna and coaxial points before signing the lease. Renters may ask the rental provider to install these items, but the rental provider does not have to agree.&nbsp;</p>
<p>Renters must get the rental provider's permission to install aerials or coaxial points. Read more about&nbsp;<a href="/housing/renting/repairs-alterations-safety-and-pets/renters-making-changes-to-the-property">renters installing fixtures and altering the rental&nbsp;property</a>.&nbsp;</p>
<h2><a name="very-high-usage-charges"></a>Very high usage charges</h2>
<p>If a renter gets a very high bill for using water, gas or electricity because there is a hidden fault at the property, the rental provider might have to pay the excess amount. These very high bills are also called excessive usage charges.</p>
<p>The rental provider only has to pay excessive usage charges if:</p>
<ul>
    <li>the renter notified the rental provider, as soon as possible, of the excessive charges and the fault that caused them</li>
    <li>the renter didn&rsquo;t cause the fault, and</li>
    <li>the service provider isn&rsquo;t responsible for the fault.&nbsp;</li>
</ul>
<p>The rental provider must also reimburse the renter for any reasonable costs incurred in diagnosing the fault by a suitably qualified tradesperson. Reasonable costs&nbsp;means&nbsp;costs that most people would think are fair.&nbsp;</p>
<p>If the renter and rental provider can&rsquo;t agree about who should pay for excessive usage charges, either one can&nbsp;<a href="https://www.vcat.vic.gov.au/">apply to VCAT</a>&nbsp;to&nbsp;make a decision.&nbsp;</p>
<h2><a name="paying-for-utilities-that-do-not-meet-efficiency-standards"></a>Paying for utilities that do not meet efficiency standards</h2>
<p>If any water, gas or electricity appliances, fixtures and fittings need to be replaced, the rental provider must replace them with <a href="/housing/renting/repairs-alterations-safety-and-pets/gas-electrical-and-water-safety-standards/efficiency-standards-for-replacement-appliances-and-fixtures">efficient&nbsp;items</a>&nbsp;that meet the Australian Government&rsquo;s&nbsp;<a href="http://www.waterrating.gov.au/">minimum efficiency standards</a>.&nbsp;</p>
<p>Rental providers must pay the ongoing utility costs until a broken or faulty appliance has been replaced with one that meets efficiency standards.</p>
<h2><a name="repayment-for-a-utilities-charge"></a>Repayment for a utilities charge</h2>
<p>If the renter pays a charge that should have been paid by the rental provider, they can ask the rental provider to pay them back using a <a href="/library/forms/housing-and-accommodation/renting/notice-to-rental-provider-of-rented-premises.docx">Notice to rental provider of rented premises (Word, 97KB)</a>. The rental provider has 28 days to repay the charge.</p>
<p>Similarly, if the rental provider pays a charge that the renter should have paid, they can ask the renter to pay them back using a <a href="/library/forms/housing-and-accommodation/renting/notice-to-renter-of-rented-premises.docx">Notice to renter of rented premises (Word, 88KB)</a>. The renter has 28 days to repay the charge. </p>
<p>You must show proof, such as a receipt, that you have made the payment when you ask to be repaid. If the parties cannot agree about who should pay, either can <a href="https://www.vcat.vic.gov.au/">apply to VCAT</a> to make a decision.</p>
<p>VCAT can also make a decision about who should pay unpaid utilities charges when they make an order about ending a rental agreement because of&nbsp;<a href="https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/resources-and-tools/family-violence">family violence</a>.</p>
<h2><a name="when-a-renter-moves-out"></a>When a renter moves out</h2>
<p>When a renter moves out, they must tell the utilities companies (including water) the date they are leaving the property. This is so the companies know when to stop charging the renter and send them a final bill.&nbsp;</p>
<p>Until a new renter moves in and the utilities companies have the new renter&rsquo;s billing details, the rental provider must pay any utility costs after the previous renter moves out.&nbsp;</p>
<h2><a name="special-rules-for-caravan-parks"></a>Special rules for caravan parks&nbsp;</h2>
<p>Caravan park residents and site tenants must pay for the installation and connection of services from a supply point on the site to their caravan or home.&nbsp;</p>
<p>You can read more information about <a href="/housing/renting/starting-and-changing-rental-agreements/different-rental-agreements/residency-in-a-caravan-park">renting in caravan parks</a> or <a href="/housing/renting/starting-and-changing-rental-agreements/different-rental-agreements/site-agreements-in-residential-parks-and-villages">renting in residential parks</a>.&nbsp;</p>
<h2>Sections of the Act</h2>
<p>If you want to know what the law says about paying for utilities and services, you can read these sections of the <a href="https://www.legislation.vic.gov.au/in-force/acts/residential-tenancies-act-1997"><em>Residential Tenancies Act 1997</em></a>:</p>
<ul>
    <li>Sections 52, 53, 53AAA, 53A, 53B, 54, 55, 56, 57 &ndash; Other charges (residential rental agreements)</li>
    <li>Sections 108, 109A &ndash; Other charges (rooming houses)</li>
    <li>Sections 120AA, 120AAB &ndash; General duties of residents and rooming house operators (rooming houses)&nbsp;</li>
    <li>Sections 162, 163, 164, 165, 166 &ndash; Other charges (caravan parks)</li>
    <li>Section 206ZE, 206ZF &ndash; Other charges (Part 4A site agreements)</li>
</ul>
